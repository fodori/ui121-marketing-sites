# Feathery: 150 online form statistics

Fetched: 2026-09-23
Status: ok

## Summary

Feathery’s 30 June 2023 post, titled as 150 statistics, is an older compilation that later articles still mine. The lead “did you know” is that about 34% of people who start a form do not finish, citing source [1] in their list. That is much lower than the 67% figures in the 2025–2026 pieces in this archive. Do not blend them. Feathery is selling form optimization, and the 34% line is the hook on this page only.

Completion after start, attributed to [1]: insurance about 95%, applications 74.51%, registration 63.37%, purchase 58.19%, inquiry 66.78%, onboarding 68.37%, contact forms only 37.85%. Contact-form encounter-to-submit is about 9.09%. Property forms average about 7.5 minutes. Mobile usage share by industry: ecommerce 25.89%, insurance 52.84%, education 59.63%, financial services 45.35%, software 36.9%, healthcare 49.78%. Half of marketers treat inbound, including forms, as the main lead source. 50% of employers think long applications filter uninterested candidates (SHRM, via their [2]). If any part of a form is hard to navigate, 67% may discard it.

Abandonment reasons match FormStory’s set: security 29%, length 27%, ads or upselling 11%, unnecessary questions 10%. Comparison forms are left after about 50 seconds, versus an average abandon time of 1 minute 43 seconds. Travel forms abandon more because they are long. About 60% of job seekers quit. 76.9% of shoppers abandon checkout. About 12% are more likely to abandon checkout without trust badges. Dropdowns have the highest abandonment. Phone numbers: about 37% leave unless optional, and making phone optional can nearly double submissions in one claim. CAPTCHA raises abandonment. Forced account creation: about 23%. Password field mean abandonment 10.5%. Email 6.4%, phone 6.3%. Industry highs: travel 81%, nonprofits 77.9%, finance 75.7%, retail 75.8%.

UX block: onboarding completion 35.33% on mobile versus 50.8% on desktop. Multi-column layouts take 15.4 seconds longer. Inline validation (they define it as checking a field as it is filled, not only on submit): 22% more completions, 31% higher satisfaction, 42% faster, 47% fewer eye fixations. Fading success messages made people think valid fields had become invalid. Address fields take about 7.4 seconds. 27% of the global online population uses mobile search, and they suggest voice for long text. Autofill: 23% triggered it, completion 71% with autofill versus 59% without. View-to-start is very high in legal services (97.63%) and local government (88.09%), and lower in ecommerce (55.94%).

The rest of the post continues the same citation style into conversion tactics and Feathery’s own product. It is a 2023 roundup. Later writers (FormStory 2024, Gnosari 2026) reuse slices of it. Treat overlaps as one family of secondary stats, not independent studies.

## Key facts

- Published 30 June 2023 by the Feathery team.
- Hook stat: ~34% of starters do not finish ([1]). Conflicts with later 67% benchmarks.
- Contact forms: ~9% of viewers submit; ~38% of starters complete.
- Trust badges: ~12% more checkout abandonment if absent.
- Security 29%, length 27%, upsell 11%, unnecessary questions 10%.
- Inline validation: +22% completion, +31% satisfaction, 42% faster.
- Mobile vs desktop onboarding completion: 35.33% vs 50.8%.
- Phone optional: cited as nearly doubling submissions in one claim.

## How it works

There is no product walkthrough in the fetched portion. The implied loop is measure field time and return rate, remove dropdowns and required phone numbers, add inline validation and trust cues, and prefer a single column. Voice is mentioned only as an input alternative for long text, not as a conversation.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Use the 12% trust-badge line and the 29/27 security-and-length split when the page needs a trust angle, and label them as Feathery’s 2023 compilation. Do not lead with 34% abandonment on a Pulsarform page that also cites 67.9%. The useful mechanism hint is inline validation and partial return: a live form can show “this field is fine” and can reopen the draft, which is closer to an inbox than a static POST. Feathery’s own free-plan limits show up more clearly in Tally’s later roundup (two live forms, 100 submissions) than here.
