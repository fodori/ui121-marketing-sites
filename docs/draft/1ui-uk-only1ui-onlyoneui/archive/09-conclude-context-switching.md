## Summary

Sian Bennett’s Conclude article (published 24 April 2025, updated 16 September 2026; the page also notes earlier versions from 2023 and 2024) treats context switching as the hidden cost underneath automation and AI. The definition is a shift of attention from one task, tool, or conversation to another. Each shift feels small. Together they erase deep work. The set piece is the “toggle tax” from a 2022 Harvard Business Review study: nearly 1,200 toggles a day between applications and websites, almost four hours a week reorienting, about five working weeks or 9% of annual work time.

Supporting studies: Asana’s Anatomy of Work Index 2022 for about 10 applications a day and about 25 switches a day. The Workgeist Report for six in ten people struggling to track information across apps, and about 59 minutes a day searching. Qatalog and Cornell for 9.5 minutes to get back into flow, 45% saying toggling makes them less productive, and 43% calling it mentally exhausting. A 2024 multitasking note is cited for up to a 10-point IQ drop and for chronic switching consuming up to 40% of productive time.

Causes: an always-on culture, too many tools (Slack, Teams, email, Zoom, project tools, documents, CRMs), and missing integration so the same work is retyped across systems. Remedies split into personal practice (deep-work blocks, batched messages, three daily priorities, OKRs) and product (Conclude Connect to bridge Slack and Teams; Conclude Apps to run tickets, incidents, and tasks inside the chat the team already uses, with AI summaries and links to Jira and Zendesk).

- Product pair: Conclude Connect (Slack ↔ Teams) and Conclude Apps (tickets inside chat).

## How it works

Switching is described as a working-memory reload: the brain drops the report, loads the chat, then reloads the tracker. Integration reduces switches by moving the task to where the conversation already is, or by syncing two chat systems so people do not duplicate threads. Automation (the post names Make and Zapier as examples) removes the manual hop. Conclude’s specific mechanism is to keep the employee in Slack or Teams and pull Jira, Zendesk, and ticket state into that window, including AI summaries so a long thread does not require a context change.

## Implications for the one-UI domains / ui121

Conclude’s bet is “work where you already are,” which is chat. ui121’s bet is “work on one canvas,” which can include a chat-originated task but does not make the thread the interface. Marketers can agree on the toggle tax and disagree on the destination. For only1ui.com, the line is that connecting Slack to Teams still leaves forms, charts, and case views in other products. A JSON UI task can be the thing the agent or the ticket renders, so the human confirms structured fields instead of reading a summary and opening three more tools. Use Conclude as the research synthesis, and keep the HBR attribution attached to the 1,200 / four-hour figures.
