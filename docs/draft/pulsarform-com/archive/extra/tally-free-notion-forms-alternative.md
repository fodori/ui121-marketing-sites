## Summary

Notion Forms, as Tally describes them, are forms bound to a Notion database. They exist on every Notion plan, can be anonymous, can be filled by people who do not have Notion, and can fire automations. Tables, charts, and boards then analyze the rows inside the workspace. The cost model is per seat: Plus at $12 and Business at $18 per seat per month, so access grows with headcount. Tally’s counter-offer is a flat $29 Pro plan with unlimited seats, on top of a free plan that already includes logic, payments, signatures, uploads, and a formula editor. Their example expression multiplies price by quantity and subtracts a discount.

They tell readers to keep Notion Forms for internal surveys that should never leave the workspace, and to use Tally for public forms, customization, and EU-hosted collection. Using both is allowed. Tally is described as a certified Notion technology partner. Two connections: paste a form URL to embed it in a Notion page, or send each response straight into a database without Zapier or Make, mapped onto Notion properties. Linked guides cover embeds, popups with Super, sync, a small store, a free CRM, audience feedback, and a set of use cases by Matthias Frank. The editor uses the same slash-command habit as Notion.

## Key facts

- Notion Forms write straight into a database on all Notion plans.
- Per-seat pricing cited: Plus $12, Business $18.
- Tally Pro: $29 flat, unlimited members.
- Native sync, no Zapier requirement.
- Embed by pasting the Tally URL.
- EU hosting called out for sensitive public forms.

## How it works

Either Notion’s own form writes a row immediately, or a Tally form embeds in the page and writes the row through a native integration. Analysis stays in Notion views. There is no shared live conversation. The database view is the inbox teams already use.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

A lot of operators already “handle” forms as a new Notion row. Pulsarform’s machine inbox has to feel that immediate, and then stay open as a thread instead of a static record. Flat versus per-seat pricing is secondary. The product lesson is that the system of record should update without a middleware bill.

> “Notion Forms for quick internal data collection, and Tally for more advanced, customer-facing forms.” — paraphrased from Tally, https://tally.so/help/tally-a-free-notion-forms-alternative
