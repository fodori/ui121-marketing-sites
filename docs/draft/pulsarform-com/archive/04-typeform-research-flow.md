## Summary

Research Flow is Typeform’s customer-research product, sold as qualitative depth at survey scale. The promise is an in-depth study in hours so a business decision does not wait on a research agency. The page is demo-gated rather than self-serve.

The mechanism is four stages. Design: tell the AI what you want to learn and it drafts objectives, a guide, and questions. Recruit: use your own customers or a verified panel, with automated screening and incentive handling. Run: many studies in parallel over text, audio, and video, with adaptive follow-ups that ask why. Analyze: summaries, themes, sentiment, and highlight reels from transcripts, ready to share in minutes.

Claimed lifts versus a plain question set: 4.5 times more feedback per question, 3 times more emotional or contextual insight, and 2 times participant engagement time. The FAQ draws a hard line against surveys. Surveys collect structured answers and stop. Research Flow mixes those questions with a live moderated conversation that probes in the moment, then surfaces themes, sentiment, quotes, and clips without a human interviewer or a synthesis sprint.

Audiences named: product marketing (concepts, messaging, positioning), product (behavior and ideas), UX and research (scaling qual), growth and strategy (trends and sentiment). Study types include concept tests, experience research, usage and attitudes, brand perception, and open qualitative dives. Participants can be a verified panel or the customer’s own list, screened with quality checks. The page is explicit that this does not replace researchers. It automates moderation, transcription, and synthesis so people spend time on judgment.

Timing: design and launch in minutes, insights in hours as answers arrive, instead of weeks of interviews.

## How it works

A team states a learning goal. The AI writes the study plan. People are screened in. Each participant hits an adaptive interview that can follow a vague or interesting answer instead of a fixed branch written in advance. As responses land, synthesis updates, so the team can decide before a traditional fieldwork calendar would have finished. The unit of work is still a study, not an ongoing customer thread.

This is the clearest 2026 proof that buyers accept “the form talks back.” Pulsarform’s version of that idea is operational, not research: every inbound submission is a live thread a machine can triage and a human can answer, not a one-off moderated panel.
