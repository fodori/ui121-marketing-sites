## Summary

Virginia Anderson’s Jotform blog post, updated 16 September 2026 and marked 17 minutes, is a vendor roundup of Formspree alternatives. The opening stat, from a Zuko Analytics report as they cite it: only 38% of people who interact with a contact form submit it, and only 9% of contact-form visitors convert. They treat form tooling as a pipeline decision, then position Formspree as the company that popularized pointing an HTML form at a URL, and list why people leave: a free plan of 50 submissions a month, API access and autoresponses on a $20-a-month Professional plan or higher, no visual builder, no e-signatures, and a developer required for changes.

Six alternatives, with prices as Jotform states them. Jotform: no-code builder, 20,000-plus templates, conditional logic, payments without an added Jotform transaction fee, spam filtering, embed code, mobile apps, Tables that turn submissions into a spreadsheet, Sign for e-signatures, and Report Builder, which they explicitly call a real-time dashboard built from submission data. They contrast that with Formspree’s inbox, which counts submissions and says little else. Free Starter: five forms and 100 monthly submissions. Paid from $34 a month for up to 25 forms and 1,000 submissions. Enterprise is unlimited, custom price.

Formcarry: server-side field rules, three thank-you pages, 1,500-plus integrations, team roles and logs on paid plans. Free: one form, 50 submissions. Paid from $5 a month, unlimited forms, 500 submissions. Web3Forms: access key by email, no dashboard required to start, hCaptcha and reCAPTCHA v3, honeypot on the free plan, custom redirect on paid plans. Free: unlimited forms, 250 submissions. Paid from $16 a month in one section and $49 a year in the summary table, so the price line is inconsistent inside the article. Getform: templates, AI text, CSS, CSV export, file upload, Zapier, HubSpot, Keap, Sheets. Free: unlimited forms, 100 submissions. Paid from $14 a month, 1,000 submissions. Basin: HTML, JavaScript, API, or a no-code builder, webhook to Sheets, Airtable, or email, notifications and spam filters. Free: one form, 50 submissions. Paid from $12.50 a month, three forms, 250 submissions. An FAQ later says Basin’s Agency plan reaches 25,000 submissions a month. Netlify Forms: attribute on the form, Akismet, honeypot, reCAPTCHA v2, dashboard storage, export, Slack or email, webhook, Zapier. Free: 300 monthly credits. Paid from $9, 1,000 credits.

Later pricing notes on Formspree itself: free 50 submissions and a 30-day archive, two notification emails, basic spam filtering, reCAPTCHA, Ajax, no custom branding or API. Personal is described as 200 submissions. Professional or Business for 2,000-plus. Personal and Professional about $10–$20 a month, Business from $60. Web3Forms Agency is cited at up to 20,000 monthly submissions.

The line that matters for this research is Jotform calling a real-time dashboard built from submissions a reason to leave a simple inbox.

## How it works

Each alternative is either a hosted POST endpoint or a full builder. Submissions are stored, emailed, and pushed to sheets or CRMs. Jotform’s differentiator in this piece is that the same vendor also draws Tables and a live report, and can collect a signature, so the team does not live only in an inbox archive. The dashboard is still analytics over rows, not a reply thread with the respondent.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Jotform has already used the phrase Pulsarform needs: a real-time surface built from the submission, set against Formspree’s passive inbox. Pulsarform can accept that contrast and move it one step. A dashboard that charts submissions is still a report. A machine inbox is where a person or an agent acts on entry number 101 before the chart updates. Use the 38% and 9% Zuko lines only with attribution, and do not repeat Jotform’s competitor prices without a date stamp. They already drifted inside one article.
