## Summary

James Vjestica (The Access Group, Not For Profit, posted 13 October 2022, modified 31 July 2026) writes for UK charities and public bodies, but the diagnosis is general. Extra workplace apps were meant to help and instead create app overload: people miss messages, actions, and deadlines because they switch all day. He cites a survey of 2,000 workers in the US, UK, and Australia: 69% waste up to an hour navigating work apps, and 68% shift between apps up to 10 times an hour. Other reports he alludes to put the daily set at 10–25 apps.

He calls app overload the dominant modern form of context switching. The brain unloads one context and loads another. One study he cites found about nine and a half minutes to get back into a good workflow after an application switch. Typical office apps — email, office files, sharing, voice, social, documents, video, messaging, HR, CRM — each have their own experience, login, and security model, and personal apps add more.

The product half is Assemble, Access’s volunteer system. Instead of a different SaaS product for recruiting, rotas, expenses, and reporting, managers switch features inside one experience. He says the product has 130-plus features across the volunteer journey, on web and mobile, updated from user feedback. He contrasts that with bolting volunteering onto a generic CRM, which he calls possible but expensive and incomplete.

Customer sketches, without hard before/after numbers: RSPCA from 2018, centralising volunteer data and recruitment and linking that to more animal rescues; Leicester City Council using Assemble in the pandemic for adverts, applications, automated welcome, scheduling, and self-serve booking; Barnardo’s using it as the home for recruitment and scheduling across 600-plus retail stores and regional service teams. A closing survey line: about two-thirds of workers believe a unified platform improves workflow (67%), productivity (65%), and makes work feel less chaotic (62%).

## How it works

The argument is substitution of navigation. A specialist full-lifecycle app keeps recruiting, scheduling, communication, expenses, and reporting in one login and one set of patterns. People still move between tasks, but they do not relearn a vendor UI or hunt a message in another inbox. Cloud delivery is how they say the product keeps up with practice. The buyer advice is to demo depth, because volunteer tools vary from point solutions to suites, and a CRM extension often fails to cover the journey.

## Implications for ui1to1.com / ui121

This is a UK-market proof that “one system” is already how buyers talk, including charities, councils, and the NHS-adjacent examples he names. ui1to1.com can borrow the pain (69%, 9.5 minutes, missed messages) and widen the cure. Assemble is one domain’s suite. ui121 is one interface per person across domains: the volunteer manager, the reviewer, and the volunteer can each get a live canvas for their part of the same work, rather than a second product.

The 67/65/62 belief figures are perception, not measured productivity. Say that. The Barnardo’s and RSPCA names are useful as “UK organisations already bought the single-home idea,” not as ui121 customers. Human-in-the-loop fits the self-serve booking story: the person acts in the interface, the process continues, and nobody retypes the decision into email.
