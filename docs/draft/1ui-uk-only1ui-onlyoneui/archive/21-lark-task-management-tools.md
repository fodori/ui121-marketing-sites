## Summary

Benjamin Grant’s Lark roundup (18 September 2026) opens on the productivity paradox: jumping between Slack, Google Drive, and Zoom wastes more time as the app count rises. The recommended escape is a unified platform that puts chat, docs, and tasks in one interface. Lark is ranked first, then Trello, Asana, ClickUp, monday.com, Wrike, Smartsheet, Todoist, Airtable, and Jira. The piece is a vendor list with Lark’s architecture described in the most detail.

Lark’s claim is that chat, video, documents, and tasks are built as one system, not integrated after the fact. Lark Base is a no-code database with Kanban, Gantt, grid, and gallery views, dashboards, and automations (notify on status change, assign from a form). Lark Tasks can be created from a sentence in a doc, a meeting, or a chat card, with deadlines on the same calendar. Messenger receives notifications from those objects so the user does not switch apps to hear that work changed. AI is described as included rather than a paid add-on: Lark Minutes for meeting summaries and action items, and enterprise search across messages, files, mail, calendars, and docs. Pricing in the table: free for up to 20 users; paid from $6 per user per month.

The other tools are summarised by the job they are best at. Trello is simple Kanban from $5. Asana is cross-functional planning from $10.99, with timelines and portfolios. ClickUp is the customisable suite from $7, including docs, whiteboards, and chat. monday.com is visual workflows from $9 with a three-seat minimum. Wrike is enterprise portfolio and proofing from $10. Smartsheet is the spreadsheet grid from $9. Todoist is personal capture from $4. Airtable is the relational database and Interface Designer from $20. Jira is agile delivery from about $7.53.

Section outline: the switching problem; a comparison table; a deep Lark review (Base, Tasks, Messenger, AI); shorter reviews of the other nine.

## Key facts

- Thesis: unified chat, docs, and tasks in one interface to stop context switching.
- Lark: free up to 20 people; paid from $6/user/month; AI summaries and cross-app search included in the description.
- Named Lark parts: Base, Tasks, Messenger, Minutes, enterprise search.
- Price floor of the set, as listed: Todoist $4, Trello $5, Lark $6, ClickUp $7, Jira ~$7.53, Smartsheet and monday.com $9, Wrike $10, Asana $10.99, Airtable $20.
- monday.com noted with a 3-seat minimum.

## How it works

A message, a document selection, or a meeting line becomes a tracked task without a second product. Status changes notify inside chat. Search treats the suite as one corpus. Automations are configured by non-developers on the database. That is in-suite consolidation: the vendor owns every object type, so handoffs are native. It does not describe rendering an external system’s form inside Lark. The opening states the paradox directly: more apps, including the hop from Slack to Drive to Zoom, means more time wasted, and a unified chat-docs-tasks interface is the proposed fix.

## Implications for the one-UI domains / ui121

Lark states the one-interface slogan in the collaboration category and then sells a suite. For 1ui.uk that slogan is the demand. ui121’s supply is different: chat, docs, and tasks are renderings of a JSON model, and so is a form or chart that does not live in Lark Base. Use the tool list to show buyers already comparison-shop for “one place,” and use the price spread to show the market is fragmented even among unifiers. The productivity paradox sentence is safe marketing language if it stays attributed as Lark’s framing, not as an independent study.
