## Summary

IBM Think defines SaaS sprawl as unchecked growth in how many cloud applications an organisation adopts and actually uses. Dan Nosowitz and Michael Goodwin argue it is usually not malice. Teams grab a tool because it solves a real problem quickly, and the pile-up produces waste, slow workflows, data silos, and security exposure. The page is an explainer: what SaaS is, the hallmarks of sprawl, the causes, the damage, and a manage-it cycle.

Two external studies carry the argument. IBM’s Institute for Business Value found that only 36% of enterprise tech executives manage cloud, data, AI, and product-engineering investments as one portfolio with shared objectives and architecture. A 2024 Productiv report is cited for 48% of enterprise applications being unmanaged — nobody clearly owns usage, security, licenses, renewals, or vulnerabilities. Zylo is cited for the spend picture: about $49 million a year across roughly 275 apps at the average organisation, with SaaS growing more than 20% a year and roughly half of licenses unused.

Hallmarks are redundancy (two apps for one job), siloed teams and data, constant switching to the next hot product, and missing integration. Causes are decentralised buying, no policy that tells people what is already licensed, a huge vendor market, freemium entry that skips IT, and a culture that does not treat unused seats as waste. Savannah Cherry (Slingshot) is quoted on good people creating the mess; James Allsop (iNet Ventures) on frictionless access producing duplicates.

Section outline: definition; why SaaS spread; hallmarks; causes; problems (money, workflow, data value for AI, training time, security); a six-step response (identify, discuss, create governance, define priorities, educate, maintain).

## Key facts

- Only 36% of enterprise tech executives (IBM IBV) manage cloud, data, AI, and engineering as an integrated portfolio.
- 48% of enterprise applications are unmanaged (Productiv, 2024, as cited).
- Average organisation spends about $49 million a year on SaaS across about 275 apps (Zylo, as cited).
- Roughly half of SaaS licenses go unused.
- SaaS annual growth cited via Zylo: over 20%.
- Shadow IT and careless integrations are framed as the security consequence, not a separate topic.

## How it works

SaaS is software hosted by a vendor and opened in a browser or thin client. The customer subscribes; the vendor patches and scales. That ease is the mechanism of sprawl: a team can start a free or cheap tool without procurement, another team buys a near-duplicate, data never joins, and AI tools then train on incomplete slices. IBM’s remedy is operational, not a new employee app: inventory what exists and which problem each tool solves, check security with IT, stand up a cross-department governance group and a management platform, write adoption rules, train people, and review continuously. Integration platforms (iPaaS) are named as the usual way products are supposed to share data — and as the thing that is missing when sprawl hurts.

> “SaaS sprawl usually isn’t the result of bad intentions—it’s often the result of good people trying to solve real problems quickly.” — Savannah Cherry, quoted by IBM Think
