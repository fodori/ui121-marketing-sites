## Summary

[ui!] Urban Software Institute, founded in 2012, sells [ui!] EASY as an entry-level software-as-a-service for cities that want a first smart-city project without a full platform procurement. The product connects municipal data, visualises it, and includes a short menu of proven use cases. The name on the page is EASY, described as an open urban data platform package. The source list’s phrase “UI-as-a-Service” does not appear in the fetched English copy. What appears is SaaS for urban data, with a cockpit UI included and operated by the vendor.

The customer opens a browser on a `urbanpulse.de` host and sees climate, energy, lighting, air, and mobility status. [ui!] installs and runs UrbanPulse (the data platform) and COCKPIT (the visualisation). The city must supply the data.

## Key facts

- Commercial shape: 12-month minimum, then monthly cancellation with 90 days’ notice. Hosting, monitoring, and cloud are in the price.
- Caps: at most two use cases from one theme and three use cases overall; three further tiles can be static text and an image. Up to 20 sensors in the entry package.
- Availability stated as 95%, excluding announced maintenance. History kept 12 months.
- Standards: DIN SPEC 91357 (open urban platforms, with Fraunhofer FOKUS). Languages: German and English. Themes: light or dark, plus a municipal logo. Weather in the sidebar. Map with sensor locations and time series.
- Themes and connectors are concrete: climate (land use, waste, CO2, energy mix), environment (Vaisala, Libelium, HLNUG, BigBelly, Mr Fill, noise, flood), energy (ChargeCloud and others, Schréder, Philips CityTouch), mobility (parking, modal split, INRIX, roadworks).
- Data stays with the municipality and is not visible to other customers. Upgrade path: full UrbanPulse and COCKPIT without losing the data. Sales: vertrieb@ui.city, Darmstadt phone number.

## How it works

The city picks use cases and provides credentials or a push endpoint for sensors that are already live. [ui!] connects those feeds, refines raw data into structured information, and shows it in COCKPIT. Role rights decide which staff or citizens see which tiles. The city does not install the platform. A fuller contract removes the sensor and tile caps.

## Implications for uiaas.uk / ui121

Someone in the UK/EU market already uses a “[ui!]” brand for a managed interface, but the product is a smart-city cockpit, not a general UI service. The overlap with ui121 is operational: the vendor hosts the screen, the customer supplies the data, and the UI is a fixed set of tiles rather than a free design tool. That is a warning and a pattern. The warning is name collision with [ui!]. The pattern is a priced, capped, operated interface with an upgrade path. `uiaas.uk` should say “UI as a service” in words this page does not, and should not imply a smart-city product.
