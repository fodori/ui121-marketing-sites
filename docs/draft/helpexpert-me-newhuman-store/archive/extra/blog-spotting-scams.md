# Spotting scams

- **Source:** https://rentahuman.ai/blog/spotting-scams-staying-safe
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-23
- **Status:** ok
- **Collection:** RentAHuman blog (sibling of source 8)

## Summary

A worker-safety post dated 1 February 2025 and updated 5 February 2026 (about seven minutes) says gig work that mixes agents and crypto attracts scams. The rules it stresses: the platform does not charge humans to join or to unlock tasks, so any “registration fee” or “equipment deposit” is a scam; nobody legitimate needs a seed phrase, private key, or wallet password, while a wallet address is safe to share; and payouts that offer hundreds of dollars for minutes of work with no effort are bait. The visible portion starts a third red flag on too-good-to-be-true pay and was cut off in the extract after that setup, but the thesis is clear: walk away when the ask feels wrong. The post is practical, not a legal policy.

## Key facts

- Humans are not charged to join.
- Never collect seed phrases; addresses are the shareable identifier.
- Outlier pay for tiny effort is treated as a fraud signal.
- Audience: workers, including people new to crypto.

## How it works

Education, not a product feature. The implied control is “money flows to the worker through the platform,” so off-platform payment requests are the tell.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

A real-time expert queue will attract the same pitches: pay us to get verified, paste a key, answer this for $500 in five minutes. Write the worker rules before launch, especially if x402 or wallets are involved. “You never pay to answer” should be as blunt as their line. Fake high bounties are also a brand risk on the buyer side; a help product should cap or review outlier prices. This is research for a deferred product, not a safety manual to ship verbatim.
