## Summary

Wikipedia describes Pearl.com as a paid online question-and-answer service based in San Francisco, and notes that the article title redirects from JustAnswer. The company began as JustAnswer in 2003, founded by Andy Kurtzig, who had earlier built and sold a classified-ad automation business. Around 2012 it was a forum where customers asked lawyers, clinicians, computer technicians, relationship counselors, and other specialists. The article says that by 2014 operations were changed to meet regulations. It is flagged as needing an update since June 2021, so later product details may be missing.

A controversy section summarizes a Wall Street Journal piece in which the founder said cheap legal answers (on the order of $30 to $40) can miss details that come out in person, while still claiming the service shortens a later appointment. A January 2026 update says the U.S. Federal Trade Commission sued JustAnswer, alleging the ads promised a $1 or $5 join fee while customers were enrolled in a monthly subscription roughly $28 to $125 that kept billing until cancelled, and that the subscription was not disclosed clearly. The article also says that as of 1 February 2026 more than 9,000 Trustpilot reports concerned the U.S. branch and almost 5,000 the UK branch, in the context of that dispute. The article is a short stub.

## Key facts

- Founded 2003 as JustAnswer by Andy Kurtzig; later branded Pearl.com; San Francisco.
- Early shape: paid questions to professionals across many fields.
- 2012-era press: legal answers discussed around $30–$40.
- January 2026: FTC civil case about subscription enrollment (alleged join fee $1 or $5; alleged monthly fee about $28–$125).
- Wikipedia’s own warning: article may be out of date (banner since 2021).

## How it works

The encyclopedia entry does not document the current UI. Historically it was a customer posting a question and a professional answering for a fee. The 2026 lawsuit description implies a subscription meter rather than a pure one-off payment. No agent API is mentioned.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

JustAnswer is the existence proof that strangers will pay for a written expert answer at consumer prices. It is also a warning about billing trust: a “cheap first question” that converts into a subscription is now a regulatory story. A later real-time network should price the answer in the open, take payment for that answer, and not hide a membership. There is still no sign, on this page, of machine clients. That remains the gap. Do not repeat their category (medical and legal advice to consumers) without a compliance plan; the interesting slice for agents is verifiable, low-stakes judgment, not unlicensed professional practice.
