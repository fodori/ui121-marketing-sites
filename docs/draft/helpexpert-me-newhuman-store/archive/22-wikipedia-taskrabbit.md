## Summary

Wikipedia describes Taskrabbit as a US marketplace that matches freelance labor with local demand: errands, furniture assembly, moving, delivery, and handyman work. It was founded in 2008 in Boston by Leah Solivan, a former IBM engineer, originally as RunMyErrand, after she needed dog food and had no time to buy it. The name changed in 2010 when the company moved toward San Francisco. An IKEA affiliate acquired it in 2017. The article says more than 200,000 independent workers use the platform. As of a June 2025 update it operated in eight countries and in all 50 US states. CEO named in the infobox is Ania Smith. The company is described as distributed, with no single headquarters, after it closed offices including San Francisco in 2022.

The history is a standard gig-marketplace arc: fbFund and an advisor role for Tim Ferriss in 2009; a $5 million Series A in 2011 when it had about 1,500 taskers and launched an iOS app; a brief outside CEO; a Series B of about $17.8 million; Solivan returning as CEO; Stacy Brown-Philpot joining from Google as COO in 2013 and becoming CEO in 2016. A 2013 “Taskrabbit Business” product took a 26% commission. London in 2013 tested a new model where taskers set rates and the first to respond got the job; that model rolled out in 2014 and caused backlash, then an app revision in 2015. In 2016 the service fee moved to 30% for all tasks and a trust fee rose from 5% to 7.5%. By January 2017 there were about 55,000 active taskers. After the IKEA deal, assembly could be booked from IKEA’s own purchase flow. A 2018 breach occurred when the article says there were about 60,000 taskers and 1.5 million users. A 2020 contractor-classification case ended in a $1.75 million award for a plaintiff group described as 10,000 taskers. In 2024 it acquired the Seattle moving company Dolly, kept as a separate brand. In 2025 it added partner pages so retailers can offer assembly and installation.

Operations: the client picks a suggested tasker by rate, skills, and availability and sends a request the tasker confirms. The platform pays the tasker, holds reviews, and passes tips through at 100% to the worker. Taskers get background checks. Criticism in the article includes fees that users say can reach about 70% of the original bid, bias research on recommendations, a happiness pledge whose exclusions made a high damage cap feel misleading, and terms that seek indemnity even if workers are later judged employees.

## Key facts

- Founded 2008 (RunMyErrand), renamed 2010, acquired by IKEA in 2017.
- More than 200,000 workers; eight countries plus all US states (as of the article’s 2025 update).
- Fee history called out: 30% service fee (2016) and a 7.5% trust fee; criticism of all-in fees up to about 70%.
- 2011: ~1,500 taskers. 2017: ~55,000 active. 2018: ~60,000 taskers, ~1.5 million users.
- CEO: Ania Smith. Founder: Leah Solivan.

## How it works

Demand is local and physical. Supply is vetted independents who set rates. Matching is suggestion plus a booking request, then in-app chat, pay, tip, and review. Retail partners (especially IKEA) can attach the same labor to a product purchase. The article does not describe a public developer API.
