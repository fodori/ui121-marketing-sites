## Summary

Nicole Wood’s Zylo post, originally June 2022 and updated 14 April 2026, argues that SaaS abundance has tipped into oversaturation: redundant and abandoned apps raise both cost and security risk. The 2026 SaaS Management Index numbers are the update. Organisations underestimate application count by 1.7× and spend by 3×. IT directly manages only 13% of SaaS on average, so 87% is bought and run by business units and individuals. The average organisation has about 305 SaaS applications; large organisations average 696. Companies add about 12 new apps every 30 days, roughly 91 a year, or 34% portfolio growth. Renewals average 211 a year — nearly one per business day.

The post then walks the damage: renewals without visibility, functional redundancy that splits purchasing power, and click-through contracts that never get negotiated into enterprise terms. Redundancy is not always wrong — a marketing stack and an IT stack may legitimately want different workflow tools — but it should be a choice after discovery, not an accident. A 2024 breach-cost figure of $4.88 million is used to justify governance, not as a Zylo-original study.

Zylo’s product answer is a lifecycle: Discover (inventory), Optimize (rationalise and drop shelfware), Plan renewals 30–90 days out, Govern intake so shadow purchases are vetted. The page is vendor content. The useful mechanism is the lifecycle, not the product pitch.

## Key facts

- App count underestimated by 1.7×; spend underestimated by 3× (Zylo 2026 SaaS Management Index).
- IT manages about 13% of SaaS; business and individuals manage the rest.
- Average organisation: about 305 SaaS apps; large organisations: about 696.
- About 12 new apps every 30 days; about 91 new apps a year (34% portfolio growth).
- About 211 renewals a year.
- Gartner figure cited in the same post: SaaS spending growing 19% to $299 billion in 2025, with 15–20% annual climb expected.
- Average data-breach cost cited for 2024: $4.88 million.

## How it works

Discovery is the base layer because finance and IT cannot see employee-bought tools. Optimisation then asks what each app is for and whether a duplicate should be merged to one standard so collaboration and negotiating power sit in one contract. Renewal planning compares license count, edition, and peer pricing before the auto-renew. Governance is an intake process so new tools are vetted instead of appearing as another login. Without that intake, the post says, shadow IT and duplicates regrow faster than any one-off cleanup.

## Implications for the one-UI domains / ui121

Zylo measures the catalogue. The one-UI domains can cite 305 / 696 and the 1.7× blind spot as proof that “we only have a few tools” is usually wrong. ui121’s distinction: even a rationalised stack of sanctioned systems still presents as many screens. Collapsing pages, forms, and charts into tasks on one canvas does not require deleting every backend. It removes the part employees feel — which app, which layout, which login — while Zylo-style governance can still own licenses underneath. The 12-apps-a-month growth rate is also the AI-era warning: if each new agent ships its own UI, the 34% yearly growth repeats at the interface layer.
