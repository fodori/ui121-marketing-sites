## Summary

Tally’s comparison argues that Typeform’s conversational look is real, and the bill is the problem. Their table says Typeform’s free plan allows unlimited forms but only 10 responses a month, while a $39 plan is where they place a 100-response tier, logos, file uploads, and payments. Tally says conditional logic, calculated fields, 10MB uploads, and Stripe payments sit on its free plan, with unlimited responses inside fair use. The editor is a document: type, do not drag. Layout can be one question per page, many questions on a page, or a mix. Typeform, in this telling, is locked to one question per page.

G2 figures they publish: Tally 4.9/5 versus Typeform 4.5; support 9.3 versus 8.4; conditional logic 9.4 versus 8.9; design flexibility 9.3 versus 9.0; payments 8.7 versus 7.6. Product Hunt is also given as 4.9. Paid ladder: Tally Pro $29 for branding, workspaces, custom CSS, drop-off insights, and partial submissions; Tally Business $89 for email verification and retention control. Typeform prices on this page run higher for branding removal ($79), analytics pixels ($129), and custom domains (enterprise, $99+). Both list a free MCP server. Tally claims a free API where Typeform’s API starts at $39. Import is OAuth and one click, including logic and structure. Quizzes can score and branch to result pages. Accessibility: semantic HTML, keyboard, VoiceOver, and NVDA on every plan.

These prices are Tally’s marketing table, and they do not always match Typeform’s own 2026 pages or Consultations’ September 2026 reading. Use them as competitive framing, not as a rate card.

## Key facts

- Typeform free cap, as Tally states it: 10 responses a month.
- Tally Pro $29, Business $89. MCP free on both, per this page.
- PDF export of submissions: Tally free, Typeform marked unavailable.
- Partial submissions: Tally $29, Typeform $39 for one submit point.
- One-click Typeform import via OAuth.
- G2: 4.9 vs 4.5 overall.

## How it works

You type blocks, optionally insert a page break to imitate Typeform, publish, and push responses through the same webhook and sheet integrations as any other Tally form. Nothing here describes a live reply thread. The fight is price and layout freedom.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

The category’s loudest argument is still “unlimited versus 10 responses,” not “who talks back.” Pulsarform can ignore the price war on the hero and use this page only to show that even the generous disruptor defines winning as more free fields. A live inbox is orthogonal to that table.
