# Headless commerce tools (Hygraph)

- Source: https://www.hygraph.com/blog/headless-commerce-tools
- Fetched: 2026-09-23
- Status: ok

## Summary

A 2026 roundup of headless commerce. The definition matches Shopify’s: the storefront is separate from catalogue, price, and checkout, and APIs feed web, apps, kiosks, and voice. The post says monolithic templates cannot keep up with speed and personalisation, and that a headless CMS should sit beside the commerce engine for guides, blogs, and landing pages.

A survey figure is given without a named sponsor in the extracted text: 78% of 1,000 businesses use a headless website architecture, and nearly 98% of the rest plan to evaluate headless within 12 months.

## Key facts

- Benefits listed: faster pages and conversion, one backend for many channels, swap a part without a replatform, easier CRM and analytics hooks, freedom to use Next.js, Nuxt, or React.
- Selection criteria: custom storefronts, API-first cart and checkout, multichannel, a headless CMS for rich content, and UIs that marketers can use.
- The piece is a buying guide. It does not replace a vendor’s own product page.

## How it works

Commerce data stays in the commerce system. Content stays in a CMS. A custom frontend composes both through APIs. Marketers edit content without taking the storefront down. Developers own the presentation.

## Implications for uiaas.uk / ui121

Headless commerce still assumes a team will build the storefront. That is the opening for a UI service on top of commerce: product data from Shopify or similar, screens from ui121, so a kiosk or app does not need its own design system. Quote the 78% figure only as this article’s survey claim.
