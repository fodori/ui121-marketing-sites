## Summary

Waymaker’s 24 February 2026 comparison (about 14 minutes) asks what “all-in-one” means once a 50-person company is paying for 12 to 16 SaaS subscriptions. The author splits the label: Google Workspace is all-in-one for communication, monday.com for work management, Zoho One for small-business operations, and each has blind spots. A genuine platform, in this framing, should cover projects, documents, goals, communication, reporting, AI, custom apps, and integrations. No vendor covers all of that well. Gartner research on digital workplace infrastructure is invoked for the claim that fewer platforms improve both productivity and security.

Eight products are scored: WaymakerOS, monday.com, ClickUp, Notion, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho One, and Airtable. WaymakerOS is described as three layers — Commander (20 built-in operational tools), Host (custom apps, agents, automations), and One (AI with workspace-wide context) — from $19 per seat per month with all 20 tools on every tier. The comparison is vendor-authored, so Waymaker’s connectedness is graded as the leader and its youth as the weakness (no built-in email, smaller marketplace).

The cost table for 25 people is the concrete artefact. WaymakerOS is estimated near $7,800 a year once email is added. ClickUp’s base is cheap and the “all-in-one” total rises once email, CRM, and goals are bought beside it (about $11,600). monday.com Pro is about $16,100 once gaps are filled. Notion about $15,400. Google Workspace about $14,200. Microsoft 365 E3 about $10,800 and may need nothing else. Zoho One about $13,500 for 45+ apps that the author says do not share context as tightly as the suite marketing implies. Airtable about $18,000 once the missing work tools are added. A Harvard Business Review gloss in the same section says context switching costs knowledge workers 20–40 minutes per day; at 25 people that is 8,000–16,000 hours a year. That is a looser citation than the 1,200-toggles study used elsewhere in this archive.

Section outline: definition of all-in-one; eight reviews; feature matrix; 25-person pricing; six criteria (breadth, depth, connectedness, AI, custom apps, and the gaps that force extra tools).

## Key facts

- Illustrative sprawl: a 50-person company on 12–16 subscriptions.
- WaymakerOS: 20 tools at every tier; from $19/seat/month; layers named Commander, Host, and One.
- monday.com: separate products (Work Management, CRM, Dev, Service); Pro example $27/seat; automations capped (250/month on Standard).
- ClickUp from $7/user/month; Notion Plus/Business in the $10–$18 band in this article’s tables.
- Zoho One: 45+ apps; $45/employee/month if the whole company is licensed.
- Microsoft 365 called the broadest single vendor, with Power Platform for custom apps and internal overlap (Planner vs Project vs Lists vs To Do).
- HBR as used here: 20–40 minutes per day lost to context switching; 8,000–16,000 hours a year at 25 people.

## How it works

The mechanism of a real all-in-one, in this article, is shared context: goals feed projects, tables feed apps, and the AI can see the workspace rather than one document. Breadth without that link is still sprawl inside one vendor — the Zoho critique. Custom app building (Waymaker Host, Microsoft Power Platform, Airtable Interfaces) is treated as the way a platform survives once off-the-shelf modules do not fit. Pricing math includes the tools you still have to buy, not the sticker of the suite alone.

## Implications for the one-UI domains / ui121

Waymaker is a migration pitch: leave the 12 tools and live in our OS. The one-UI domains should not copy that. Leaders already say “one platform” and mean “ours.” ui121’s whitespace is one rendering surface over the apps a company keeps. Use the 25-person cost table to show that “all-in-one” sticker prices hide the rest of the stack, and use the connectedness criterion as the design bar: a canvas only helps if a form, a chart, and a task share one model. The 20–40 minutes/day line can be used only with Waymaker’s wording (per day, HBR “consistently shows”), not swapped for the per-task or per-toggle figures in other notes.
