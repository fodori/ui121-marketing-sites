## Summary

Prolific sells real human data that researchers and AI teams can collect quickly. The homepage headline is “Real human data. Ready in minutes.” Three audiences are split on the page: teams evaluating and training AI (preference tuning, safety evals, benchmarks from representative people), academic and product researchers, and participants who join paid studies with a flexible schedule.

Social proof is “thousands of organizations.” Case blurbs include a HUMAINE framework about human preference for language models (arguing that technical benchmarks miss the real world and that many human evals use narrow samples), Allen Institute work that cut human data collection from weeks to hours for multimodal models, an Ivy League climate-choice study, and the Collective Intelligence Project gathering voices from 70+ countries for AI governance. The footer reveals a product surface closer to agents than the headline suggests: a human-feedback API, agent workflows, agentic evals, domain experts for healthcare and coding, a Prolific MCP, and a GitHub presence, plus a research lab and managed services. This fetch did not open those subpages. They are named capabilities, not a documented real-time Q&A marketplace.

> “Real human data. Ready in minutes.” — Prolific homepage

## Key facts

- Buyers: AI evals, academic research, market research. Sellers: study participants.
- Claimed speed: minutes, with a case study of weeks compressed to hours (Ai2).
- Named products in navigation: Human Feedback API, Agent Workflows, Prolific MCP, domain-expert pools.
- Geographic color in one story: 70+ countries.

## How it works

A customer launches a study or feedback task. Verified participants complete it and are paid. The page stresses sample quality and representativeness more than chat. Agent-facing APIs and an MCP connector are advertised as ways to pull that same participant pool into model workflows. Nothing on the homepage describes bidding, escrow for a single errand, or an open browse of named experts.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

Prolific is the batch, science-flavored version of “rent a human,” and it is already growing an agent socket. “Ready in minutes” is language a knowledge network will want, but their minute is study recruitment, not a live expert on the line. If helpexpert launches later, expect Prolific to be the incumbent for evals and panels, not for “what is the price of this item on this street.” Do not claim their participant pool. Do claim a different object: one accountable person, one answer, one payment, with a UI a human and an agent can both read. Their MCP mention means “we will be the only human MCP” is already false.
