## Summary

The homepage and the FAQ URL both stopped on a Cloudflare check (HTTP 403, then a bot interstitial). Indexed text from GLG’s own FAQ (`/who-we-are/faq/`) and from a GLG article on what an expert network is was readable the same day, and this note uses only that. It is not a full page capture.

GLG describes itself as an insight network, founded in 1998, that it calls the first of the category. Clients bring a question while they explore a strategy, product, segment, or direction. GLG matches them with people who have done the work. The forms it names are a direct call, a survey, bespoke research, events, content, or a longer engagement. Matching is described as proprietary data plus a human team: suggest experts, then look for background problems, quality issues, or conflicts. Every expert must train and accept the policies. A compliance team and tools are part of the pitch, aimed at investors and other clients who cannot receive confidential or material non-public information.

The expert-network explainer adds the industry frame. These firms exist so a client can buy first-hand insight that is not in a report. GLG says the category reached about $1.3 billion in revenue by the end of 2023. Most networks serve finance. GLG says it also serves technology, pharma, devices, consumer goods, consultants, and litigators, from practitioners to executives. The standard product is a one-to-one phone consultation. An example on that page is a client closing a research gap on alternative proteins by talking to people in that market. A third-party review (not GLG’s site) describes calls of about 30–60 minutes, surveys, and an AI summary after a call. Those review details are not treated as GLG’s own claims.

Nothing in the retrieved GLG text describes an agent API, a per-question micropayment, or an MCP tool. The buyer is a professional with a research project. The wait is scheduling a screened call, not a runtime pause.

## How it works

A client states the question. GLG’s matching suggests people with that experience, then screens conflicts and quality. The usual session is a private call. Surveys and longer projects exist when one voice is not enough. Experts are paid for the engagement. The client pays GLG. The transcript or the conversation is the deliverable. AI, where GLG mentions it on the calls page in search text, is a tool on top of the call (summaries, staying inside the client’s compliance rules), not a way for a software agent to hire the expert.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

GLG is the slow, expensive, human-buyer end of “pay a person who knows.” It validates that organisations already pay for one question answered by a screened human, and that compliance is the product as much as the answer. It does not validate a real-time agent socket. helpexpert.me would be the opposite latency and the opposite client: a machine asks, a person answers now, without a project manager booking a 45-minute call. Do not claim GLG has or lacks an agent API until the live site can be opened. The indexed pages simply never mention one.
