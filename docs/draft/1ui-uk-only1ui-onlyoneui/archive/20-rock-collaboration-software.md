## Summary

Rock’s 29 April 2026 guide says the average team ran 5 to 7 collaboration apps in 2023 and that by 2026 most teams are consolidating to 2 or 3. The author is explicit about bias: Rock is an all-in-one chat and project workspace. The market is split into two schools. All-in-one products (Rock, ClickUp, Notion, Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace, Basecamp) replace several specialists with one login and one search index, and give up some depth. Best-of-breed stacks (Slack, Asana, Zoom, Miro, plus docs and billing) keep the best tool in each slot and pay in subscriptions and context switching — often 4 to 7 products.

The global team-collaboration software market is put at about $27.9 billion in 2025, with Microsoft Teams at about 37% share. Consolidation to two or three tools does not always mean buying an all-in-one; it can mean dropping the weak four and keeping three specialists. Pricing snapshots (annual, reviewed August 2026 in the article’s own caveat): Rock $89 a month flat for unlimited users; ClickUp from $7 per user; Notion from $10; Teams from about $6 inside Microsoft 365 with Copilot as a $30 add-on; Slack from about $7.25; Asana from about $10.99; Zoom from about $13.33; Miro from $8. An agency example: Slack plus Asana plus Drive plus Loom at 15 people around $8,000 a year versus Rock at $899. Rock also advertises MCP access to assistants such as Claude and Cursor on the free plan.

## Key facts

- 5–7 collaboration apps per team in 2023; consolidation toward 2–3 by 2026 (Rock’s market claim).
- Collaboration software market about $27.9 billion in 2025; Teams about 37% share.
- Best-of-breed overhead described as 4–7 subscriptions plus “which conversation lives where.”
- Rock Unlimited: $89/month flat, unlimited internal users and external clients; annual example $899.
- 15-person specialist stack example: about $8,000 a year versus that flat price.
- Native AI is mostly an add-on or a higher tier (ClickUp Brain $9/user, Slack AI $10/user, Copilot $30/user).

## How it works

An all-in-one keeps chat, tasks, notes, and files in one project space so a client thread does not jump tools. A best-of-breed stack wires specialists with integrations and accepts the seam. Rock’s mechanism is flat pricing plus guest clients in the same space, with an API and MCP so an agent can see the workspace without an enterprise tier. The article’s decision rule is team size, current app count, and whether the company is already inside Microsoft or Google.
