# Fiverr homepage

- **Source:** https://www.fiverr.com/
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-23
- **Status:** ok

## Summary

Fiverr’s homepage loaded on this date as a freelance marketplace. The title calls it a place to find global talent. Most of the extracted text is the service taxonomy, not a manifesto: graphics, programming, marketing, video, writing, audio, business, finance, AI, and consulting, each broken into dozens of packaged offers (“gigs”). Newer labels in the menu include AI agents, AI websites and apps, generative-engine optimization, data-model training, and AI consulting. There is also a “Fiverr Pro” entry, seller signup, and “let us manage your project” on several branches.

The page does not, in this extract, explain escrow, fees, or an agent API. It also does not show the old $5 price in the navigation dump. What it does show is a human-facing catalog of productized services, organized for browsing and search, with AI as a category of gigs rather than as the buyer. An earlier research pass reported the site blocking scrapers; this fetch succeeded and returned the menu. Treat the result as the public category map, not as a full marketing narrative.

## Key facts

- Marketplace of packaged freelance services, seller and buyer accounts.
- Taxonomy spans design, code, marketing, video, writing, legal, finance, and a growing AI-services branch.
- AI agents appear as something a human can buy, not as a client the site invites.
- No public agent quickstart in the extracted homepage text.

## How it works

A buyer searches or browses categories, opens a gig, and orders a defined service from a freelancer. The homepage emphasizes discovery and category breadth. Project-managed options sit beside self-serve gigs. Nothing in the extract describes programmatic posting.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

Fiverr is the catalog of remote skilled work, priced as products. It is a poor socket for an agent: the interface is a menu of gigs, and RentAHuman’s own writing (source 8) says scraping it breaks terms. A knowledge network should not launch as “gigs, but for questions” or it will look like a thin Fiverr category. The useful contrast is productized scope versus an open question with a deadline. If AI-agent gigs on Fiverr grow, that is humans selling agent-building, which is the inverse of agents hiring humans. Keep that distinction in the positioning. The product is still deferred; do not build a gig clone to answer this page.
