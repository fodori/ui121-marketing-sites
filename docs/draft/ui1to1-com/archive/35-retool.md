## How it works

The page does not document an architecture. The implied loop is: describe or generate an internal app on Retool’s builder, ship it with enterprise controls already in place, and run a portfolio of those apps under one operating model. IT is the control plane (visibility, governance). Business teams are the builders. Case metrics are offered as evidence that generated apps replace slow custom development in operations, not only in demos. Agent governance is gestured at in the article titles: someone must watch agents, and generation without oversight is the risk the hero line (“secure your vibe-coded apps”) is answering.

## Implications for ui1to1.com / ui121

Retool is the incumbent category for AI-built internal tools: faster apps, with a governance story so enterprises will allow them. ui1to1.com should not pretend that category does not exist. The distinction is what “one interface” means. Retool’s story is many internal apps, generated quickly, managed as a fleet. ui121’s story is one live canvas per person, described as data, always connected, where every workflow — including an agent that needs a human — shows up as a task, not as another app to govern.

The Ramp and DoorDash figures are Retool’s customer claims; attribute them, do not generalise. The governance headlines are the overlap with human-in-the-loop: generated software still needs a person who can see and stop what agents do. That person needs a surface. JSONUI is a different bet from a vibe-coded app builder — pre-defined blocks and a server-described UI, personal to the user, rather than a new internal application per workflow. The single interface is the competitive sentence against a portfolio of generated apps.
