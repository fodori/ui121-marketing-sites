## Summary

Tally’s homepage is the generous disruptor: the simplest way to make forms, free, and edited like a document. It claims to power 500,000-plus teams. You type questions the way you would in a doc, insert blocks the way you would in Notion, and you do not need an account to try it. Unlimited forms and submissions stay free inside a fair-use line the page mentions but does not quantify here.

Privacy is a headline, not a footer. The company is Belgian, GDPR-compliant, hosted in Europe, no cookie tracking, encryption in transit and at rest. Enterprise retention control is called out as a Business feature.

Input blocks cover contact fields, payments, e-signatures, ratings and rankings, file uploads, and date-time. Smart behavior: conditional logic (show, hide, branch, calculate), a calculator for scores and prices, hidden fields via URL parameters (the example is a name query on a tally.so/r link), answer piping, email notifications, redirect on completion, prefill, reCAPTCHA, and partial submissions.

Design: themes or custom colors, fonts, and buttons; columns; custom CSS; logo and cover; embeds such as YouTube, Calendly, and Maps; single or multi-page; 50-plus languages for default messages. Distribution: embed (including Notion, WordPress, Webflow, Framer), popup, a Tally link, or a custom domain like forms.yourdomain.com/feedback.

Integrations are push-out: Notion, Google Sheets, Airtable, webhooks, Slack, Coda, Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, and Zapier, Make, or Pipedream for the long tail. Audiences are split into creators, product, marketing, HR, office, and personal, each with three example jobs (sell, newsletter, contact; feedback, feature requests, research; leads, registration, satisfaction; engagement, applications, exit; events, help desk, suggestions; quiz, RSVP, volunteers).

Quotes on the page: Ben Lang (angel investor, previously Notion) on loving Tally; Natan Castiel (Head of Growth, Gelt) comparing Tally’s effect on forms to Notion’s effect on docs; Steven Tey (Dub.co, previously Vercel) preferring Tally to Typeform; Jac on constant features; Shyam Verma on power, prefill, and free. FAQ: born from frustration with expensive builders; advanced features (logic, signatures, calculations, uploads) are free; one-click import exists; comparisons point at Typeform, Jotform, Paperform, and others.

## Key facts

- 500,000+ teams. Unlimited forms and submissions on the free plan, fair use.
- EU-hosted, GDPR, no cookie tracking, encrypted in transit and at rest.
- Editor model: type like a document, Notion-style blocks.
- Partial submissions, calculator, hidden fields, answer piping, payments, signatures.
- 50+ languages. Custom domains. Embeds including Calendly and Maps.
- Native syncs: Notion, Sheets, Airtable, Slack, Coda, webhooks, analytics pixels.
- No account required to try. Import from other builders in one click (FAQ).

## How it works

You type a form, publish a link or embed, and each submission is stored and optionally pushed to a sheet, a database, Slack, or an HTTP endpoint. Partial submissions capture people who leave early. The respondent still finishes into a thank-you or a redirect. The operator’s ongoing work happens in Notion or Sheets, not in a Tally thread described on this page.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Tally won the “free and feels like a doc” argument. Pulsarform should not reopen it. Borrow the doc-like build metaphor only if the product actually edits that way, and spend the page on what Tally’s integration row admits: webhooks and Slack are how submissions become events. Pulsarform’s difference is that the event has a home.

Fair-use unlimited is a pricing weapon, not a liveness weapon. Mention it only to show the category’s free baseline, then leave pricing off the hero.
