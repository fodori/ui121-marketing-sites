## Summary

Jill Romford’s agilityPortal guide (14 July 2026) defines app fatigue as the drain from switching workplace applications, not from any one product being bad. A typical morning in the piece runs through email, Teams or Slack, SharePoint, an HR system, a project tool, and a company announcement — five or six tools before the needed fact appears. The argument is to consolidate everyday communication, documents, knowledge, and routine employee tasks into one internal-comms platform, and to leave specialist software alone.

Cited loads: IDC says knowledge workers spend about 8.8 hours a week searching across systems (more than a working day). Harvard Business Review research is cited for nearly 1,200 app and website switches a day and almost four hours a week recovering focus, framed as about five working weeks a year. A RingCentral workplace survey is cited for 69% of employees wasting up to an hour a day navigating apps, and 66% preferring a single communication platform. Enterprise Strategy Group is cited for 44% of organisations using 6–10 communication and collaboration platforms and 37% using 11–20. The comfort band Romford offers is three to five core workplace apps before productivity drops.

She is explicit that one app for everything is the wrong slogan. MetaTrader, CAD, Premiere, EHR systems, and IDEs stay. The fatigue is “everything around” those systems: announcements, chat, files, projects, HR requests, recognition, policies. The platform should be a front door — news, documents, people search, chat, everyday tasks, and links into Salesforce or the HR system — not a replacement for the systems of record.

Section outline: definition; how many apps is too many; hidden costs (switching, scattered information, notifications); damage to communication and onboarding; a warning-sign table; what not to consolidate; buyer checklist (news hub, messaging, workspaces, documents, knowledge, recognition, surveys, automation, mobile, integrations, reporting).

## Key facts

- About 8.8 hours a week searching for information (IDC, as cited).
- About 1,200 switches a day and almost four hours a week of recovery (HBR, as cited).
- 69% waste up to an hour a day navigating apps; 66% would prefer one communication platform (RingCentral, as cited).
- 44% of organisations use 6–10 collaboration platforms; 37% use 11–20 (ESG, as cited).
- Suggested comfort range: three to five core workplace applications.
- Specialist tools named as out of scope: trading platforms, CAD, video editors, EHRs, developer IDEs.

## How it works

Fatigue is explained as mental reload: every login, notification, and screen change forces a pause, and nobody knows which copy of a policy is current. The product pattern is a digital workplace that centralises comms and knowledge and deep-links to business systems, with versioned documents, mobile access, and light automation for approvals and onboarding. Outcomes she lists are less searching, fewer missed announcements, faster new-hire ramp, and higher trust in “where information lives.”
