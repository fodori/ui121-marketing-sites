## Summary

Speakwise’s 10 June 2026 companion piece is a sixteen-statistic survey of productivity tools. The lead contrast is blunt: companies use about 106 SaaS applications, yet workers lose up to 40% of productive time to context switching. They also say the average enterprise runs 1,000-plus apps, 79% of employees say the company has done nothing about tool fatigue, global SaaS spending hits $300 billion in 2025, and context switching costs the U.S. economy about $450 billion a year. The average focused session, in the closer they preview, has shrunk to 13 minutes.

Each item names an upstream source. BetterCloud is their source for 106 SaaS tools and the claim that most do not integrate, so people move data by hand. Conclude.io is the source for “up to 40%” of productive time (3.2 hours of an eight-hour day) and for 45% feeling less productive plus 43% exhausted by switching. Lokalise supplies 33 switches a day, 17% over 100, the 79% “no consolidation” figure, 51 minutes a week (44 hours a year) of tool fatigue, more than one in five losing 2+ hours a week, and 45% overwhelmed by alerts. Asana is their source for enterprises above 1,000 apps and poor interoperability. Hostinger: $300 billion SaaS in 2025, 18.7% compound growth through 2030. Reclaim.ai: about $450 billion U.S. cost, and over $4.5 million a year at a 1,000-person knowledge-worker company. Qatalog and Cornell, via Conclude: 9.5 minutes to regain flow; they illustrate 33 switches times even 5 minutes as more than 2.5 degraded hours. ActivTrak: AI use at work from 53% to 80%, but only 3% spend an “optimal” 7–10% of hours in AI tools, and 57% spend under 1%; focused sessions average 13 minutes 7 seconds, down 9% from 2023. Ronspot: half of businesses use 17 disconnected work platforms, only 4% are fully integrated, and 37% employ 11 or more people just to collate operational data. NextPlane, citing a 2024 study: heavy multitasking linked to about a 10-point IQ drop, which they compare to missing a night of sleep. Qrvey: AI embedded in 80% of enterprise applications by 2026, up from under 5% in 2024.

Their conclusion is the tool paradox: spend and app count rise, focus falls. The fix they argue is fewer, better-integrated tools. The last section sells voice capture into Notion.

## Key facts

- ~106 SaaS apps per company (BetterCloud); 1,000+ at the average enterprise (Asana, as cited).
- Up to 40% of productive time lost to context switching; 33 switches/day typical; 17% over 100.
- U.S. context-switching cost they cite: ~$450 billion/year; ~$4.5 million per 1,000 knowledge workers.
- Global SaaS ~$300 billion in 2025.
- 79% say the employer has not reduced tool fatigue; 45% say too many apps make them less productive; 45% overwhelmed by notifications.
- Focus session: 13 minutes 7 seconds (ActivTrak), down 9% from 2023.
- AI adoption 80% of employees, with most spending under 1% of time in the tools.
- Half of businesses on 17 disconnected platforms; 4% fully integrated.
- AI features expected in 80% of enterprise apps by 2026.

## How it works

Like their overload roundup, this is a secondary statistic list. The mechanism they describe is fragmentation: each tool solves one job and adds a login, an interface, and a notification channel. Disconnection then creates a second workforce whose job is to reconcile reports. AI embedded inside every app, they warn, can deepen the problem if it adds features without removing surfaces. The “most productive tool eliminates three others” line is the selection rule.
