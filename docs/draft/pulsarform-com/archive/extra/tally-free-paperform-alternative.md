## Summary

Paperform, in this Tally comparison, is a paid builder whose forms behave like landing pages, with serious payments and no free tier. A 14-day trial leads to $29 a month. That entry plan allows unlimited forms but, they say, only 100 submissions, with extra packs of 250 submissions for $7.50 up to a 750 cap. Webhooks sit on higher tiers around $59 and $129. Branding removal, custom domains (plus about $20 per domain each month), custom CSS, partial submissions, visit insights, and PDF export are placed at $59. GDPR and SOC 2 apply on all plans. SSO is higher up. The editor uses a slash command, but the canvas is not the live form, so authors live in preview. Logic appears only when a question is opened. The product UI is English, with respondent-facing translations including right-to-left languages.

Tally’s free plan is the contrast: unlimited responses, logic, signatures, payments, and webhooks. Pro at $29 is the same sticker as Paperform’s entry tier and, they argue, a larger bundle. Business at $89 adds retention controls the Paperform column lacks. n8n, Linear, and a Framer plugin are marked Tally-only. Paperform’s remaining win is the mini-site design and zero added commission on payments from $29.

## Key facts

- No Paperform free plan. Trial 14 days. Entry $29 with a 100-submission cap, per this page.
- Extra submissions sold in 250-packs for $7.50.
- Webhooks are not on Paperform’s entry tier here. They are free on Tally.
- SOC 2 and GDPR on every Paperform plan.
- Build view differs from the published form.
- Custom domain: $59 plus about $20 per domain per month.

## How it works

Paperform publishes a designed page that can charge and book. Tally publishes a typed form and emits events. Both finish at submission plus an integration. Partials exist on both, behind a paid plan. No operator thread is described.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Paperform already claims the form can run a business, and it still stores a one-way submit. Pulsarform can keep that ambition and change the mechanism: the submission stays open as a case. Webhooks as an upsell show that even landing-page builders treat live push as a premium pipe.
