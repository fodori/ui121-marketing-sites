## Summary

SAP’s Fiori principles page is short and canonical. Fiori is presented as one consistent experience across SAP software: visually calm, easy, and the same interaction patterns on every device. The philosophy is five principles: role-based, adaptive, simple, coherent, and delightful.

Role-based means the surface shows what that job needs, when it is needed, based on how people actually work. Delightful means the experience should let someone do the job rather than fight the software. Coherent means a sales order, a KPI review, and a leave request share one visual and interaction language. Simple means essential functions are obvious and the person can personalise down to relevant tasks. Adaptive means the same work on any device, with information shaped for quick insight.

The page does not include metrics, a component catalogue, or implementation steps. It is the principle statement inside the SAP design system (the URL sits under an iOS-oriented Fiori design-system path). The same five words are widely repeated in SAP’s public design material. No permissive content license is stated on the fetched page.

## Key facts

- Five principles: role-based, adaptive, simple, coherent, delightful.
- Examples of one language across jobs: fulfil a sales order, review KPIs, manage leave.
- Adaptive explicitly includes device independence and instant insight.
- Simple includes personalisation toward the tasks that matter to that user.
- The page is a principle statement, not a case study and not a metric.

## How it works

Fiori’s mechanism is a design system applied across modules that used to feel like separate applications. A person in a role gets a task-sized slice — the order, the KPI, the leave request — rendered with the same patterns, so learning one interaction teaches the next. Adaptation is responsive presentation of relevant facts, not a different product per device. Coherence is the constraint that stops each module team from inventing a new UI.

## Implications for the one-UI domains / ui121

Fiori is the enterprise precedent that a coherent, task-centric surface beats a different app per module. SAP did it inside SAP. ui121’s claim on 1ui.uk is the same five principles without the suite boundary: role-based tasks, one coherent component language, simple job-sized screens, adaptive layout, and a result that feels like doing the work. Pages, forms, and charts are the task types. The JSON canvas is how coherence is enforced when the backends are not all SAP. Marketers can say “Fiori proved the principles; the one UI is those principles as a renderer any system can target.” Do not imply SAP endorses that extension.
