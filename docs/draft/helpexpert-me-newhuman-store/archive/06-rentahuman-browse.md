# RentAHuman browse directory

- **Source:** https://rentahuman.ai/browse
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-23
- **Status:** ok

## Summary

Browse is a public directory of people an agent can hire. The page headline is about finding “meatspace workers,” and it offers an AI matcher (“ask renta to find a human”). On this fetch the list was biased toward people eligible in the United States, with a control to remove that country priority. No account is required to look.

Profiles show a display name, a short pitch, city, a remote flag, skill tags, a star rating and review count when present, an “active this week” badge on some cards, and an hourly rate. The visible rates clustered at $7.25 an hour, with others at $25 and $50, and at least one listing at $0.28. Skills on the first screen mixed physical work (moving, animal care, carpentry, delivery) with remote knowledge work (AI training, data annotation, web-app QA, bug reports, Excel, counseling, writing). Many cards were marked remote even when a US city was shown. Verification and portfolio markers appear on some cards, but this snapshot was not a clean “experts only” list.

Individual names from the directory are omitted here. The useful fact is the shape of the supply: a large, uneven pool, a low default rate, mixed skill quality, and activity badges instead of a live “available now” clock.

## Key facts

- Public browse; US eligibility was prioritized and removable.
- Matcher prompt: ask the product to find a human.
- Rate band observed: $0.28, $7.25 (common), $25, $50 per hour.
- Signals: stars, review counts, “active this week,” remote, skill tags, occasional portfolio.
- Supply mix: errands and trades plus AI-training and QA profiles.

## How it works

A human fills a profile with skills, place, and rate. Posters and agents filter or ask the matcher. “Active this week” is a recency badge, not a guarantee the person will answer in the next few minutes. Hiring itself continues off this page, into a message, bounty, or booking.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

The directory proves supply will show up for low hourly rates, including people who already sell judgment-like tasks (annotation, QA, counseling). It also shows why a help product cannot simply mirror this list. A $7.25 default and a week-level activity badge will not support “answer in five minutes from a verified expert.” If helpexpert or newhuman.store launches later, the browse surface should lead with availability now, domain, and answer quality — and should hide or separate gig-labor profiles so the brand does not look like a general labor board.
