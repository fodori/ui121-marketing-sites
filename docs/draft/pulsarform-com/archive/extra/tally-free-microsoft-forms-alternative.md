## Summary

Microsoft Forms, in this comparison, is the form tool you already have if the company bought Microsoft 365, and a poor fit if it did not. There is no meaningful standalone free plan. Business Basic is cited around $6 per user per month. Admins can stop people outside the tenant from responding. The editor stacks question cards on one page, with preset themes only. Multi-page layouts, columns, logos, payments, answer piping, general calculations, redirects, webhooks, and MCP are marked absent. Branching is simple section show-and-hide. Uploads go to OneDrive and stay organizational. Slack and Zapier mean Power Automate. The spreadsheet is Excel, not Google Sheets. A Graph API exists for developers.

Tally’s offer is an email login, a public link, Stripe on the free plan, and direct connections to Notion, Airtable, Sheets, and Slack. Scores they publish put Tally at 4.8 versus 4.4 overall, support 9 versus 8.1, setup 9.5 versus 9.1, logic 9.4 versus 8.3, and design 9.3 versus 7.9. There is no importer. You rebuild the form, or you ask Tally’s AI. SharePoint is not a destination. GDPR for Microsoft depends on the tenant region. Tally’s default is EU hosting. Scored quizzes with custom endings are free.

## Key facts

- Tied to Microsoft 365. External respondents can be blocked by policy.
- No webhooks, payments, multi-page flow, or MCP on this table.
- Power Automate is how data leaves the tenant.
- Tally needs no Microsoft account for authors or respondents.
- No direct migration tool.
- Design flexibility scores cited: 9.3 versus 7.9.

## How it works

A Microsoft form stays inside the tenant unless someone builds a flow. A Tally form is a public link plus optional sync. Both capture once. The interesting constraint is the org boundary, not the question types.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Customer-facing intake is where Microsoft Forms gets awkward. Pulsarform should sound like a public front door, not an intranet poll. Power Automate is the tax people pay to turn a form into a queue. The inbox should remove that tax.
