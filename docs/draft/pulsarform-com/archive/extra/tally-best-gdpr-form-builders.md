## Summary

Marie Martens, 5 August 2026, argues that a form is a personal-data process if it collects names, emails, phones, or even IP addresses, including for companies outside the EU. Sensitive data such as health adds further duties. She quotes Jacob Kalvo on collecting only what the purpose justifies, and on consent that is freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous. Pre-ticked boxes and bundled purposes are called non-compliant. Fines are mentioned only as “up to” a figure the extract did not keep intact, so the number is omitted here.

Her buyer checklist: encryption in transit and at rest, export and deletion, a DPA, an obvious retention policy, and EU storage as recommended rather than legally mandatory in every case. Tally is the free, Belgium-built default, with automatic deletion on the $89 Business plan. Typeform stores in the US by default, with transfer safeguards, and puts EU data-center choice on Enterprise from about $99. Jotform offers GDPR tools on the free plan (5 forms, 100 submissions) and residency on enterprise. A developer-oriented builder in the extract (limits of 5 forms and 500 submissions on free) is praised for regional hosting. Another vendor hosts on Azure, with field encryption at $39 and deeper security at $129. A Polish survey tool stores in the EU, caps free use at 25 responses, and starts standard plans near $99.

## Key facts

- Updated 5 August 2026. Author: Marie Martens.
- GDPR applies to EU residents’ personal data regardless of the company’s country.
- Consent must be unbundled and unticked by default, per the quoted advisor.
- Tally: EU default. Retention automation at $89.
- Typeform: US default, EU region on Enterprise.
- Jotform: GDPR tools on free, residency on enterprise.
- EU storage is recommended, not described as always mandatory.

## How it works

Pick a processor that will sign a DPA, encrypt, and delete on a schedule. Put an explicit consent field on the form. Do not hide extra purposes in one checkbox. The form itself is still a collector. Compliance is storage and paperwork, not a live conversation.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

A machine inbox holds the same personal data as a form backend, plus the thread that follows. Pulsarform copy should promise a purpose limit, a way to export or delete the thread, and a clear region story. EU readers will compare that with Tally’s Belgium default. Do not invent a fine amount or a residency claim the product does not have.

> “Businesses must collect only that information which has a proper justification for the intended purpose.” — Jacob Kalvo, quoted by Tally, https://tally.so/help/best-gdpr-form-builders
