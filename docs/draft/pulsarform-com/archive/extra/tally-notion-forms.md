## Summary

This landing page repeats the Notion partnership in shorter form. Tally is the free, unlimited builder. Forms embed in Notion, and a native integration writes responses into a database so Zapier or Make is unnecessary. Notion’s own forms are acknowledged and then set aside for teams that need logic, payments, signatures, or duplicate blocking, with a slash-command editor that feels like Notion. The page points at a comparison guide and at practical recipes: popups with Super, a store, audience feedback, and Matthias Frank’s seven use cases. The integration claim is that it maps to Notion database properties and will keep up as Notion adds properties. Competitors, they say, charge for the same sync or cap submissions. You can try Tally without an account.

## Key facts

- Native Notion database sync, no Zapier fee.
- Embed by URL.
- Positioned above Notion’s built-in forms for public and advanced cases.
- Property mapping claimed to track new Notion property types.
- Free, including the integration.

## How it works

Publish a form, paste it into a Notion page or connect the database, and each submit becomes a row. People triage in Notion. Tally is the front end. Notion is the system of record.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Confirms the pattern Pulsarform has to beat or join: form in, row out, work happens elsewhere. If ui121 is the system of record, the row should be a task thread, not only a database line. Notion sync can remain an export, not the product.
