## Summary

Typeform’s homepage sells forms as a revenue system, not a data bucket. The lead claim is that familiar conversational forms now sit next to AI automation, so a team can collect answers, run research, and manage a customer lifecycle in one product. The page splits the offer into three motions: intelligent forms that adapt per respondent, a newer Growth Flow that segments and follows up, and a newer Research Flow that runs AI-moderated studies.

The collection story is scale plus completion. Typeform says it gathers more than 48 million responses a month, and that its AI forms are proven to pull in 3.5 times more data. A separate “high response rate” block says conversational logic that adapts to every answer doubles completion versus traditional forms. Deeper answers come from video and audio, plus AI follow-ups that change as someone fills the form. Analytics covers topics, sentiment, respondent comparison, and where people drop off.

After the form, the page’s own phrase is that the flow begins. Growth Flow is framed as proactive automation: enrich a lead, build a segment, and send a personalized message when an opportunity appears. Instant capture includes e-signatures, calendar booking through Google Calendar and Calendly, and payments through Stripe and PayPal. Enrichment match rates are quoted at up to 92% for B2B and 71% for B2C. Follow-up is instant across email, SMS, and other tools, triggered by a submission or a contact update.

Research Flow is the sharpest signal on the page. It builds the study, runs thousands of AI-moderated interviews at once, and analyzes the findings. Insights are promised in hours rather than weeks, across text, video, and voice, with tone and hesitation included. Recruitment uses 400-plus targeting criteria and built-in incentives.

Proof points are customer outcomes, not product mechanics: SmartBug Media up 40% in sales leads from one form, Double Denim Marketing at $3.67 million in sales, and Viva cutting time-to-hire by 75%. The company schema on the page dates the firm to 2012 in Barcelona, founded by Robert Muñoz and David Okuniev. The organization description still leads with beautiful interactive forms and more responses, which is the older brand, while the hero has moved to AI automation.

## Key facts

- More than 48 million responses collected monthly.
- AI forms claimed to collect 3.5x more data; conversational logic claimed to double completion versus traditional forms.
- Enrichment match rates: up to 92% B2B, 71% B2C.
- Research Flow: thousands of simultaneous AI-moderated text, video, and voice interviews; 400+ targeting criteria.
- Named outcomes: +40% sales leads (SmartBug), $3.67 million sales (Double Denim), −75% time to hire (Viva).
- In-form actions: e-sign, Calendly / Google Calendar, Stripe, PayPal.
- Product split: Intelligent Forms, Growth Flow, Research Flow.
- Founded 2012, Barcelona.

## How it works

A marketer describes or designs a form. Interaction stays conversational and can branch, then AI can ask a follow-up. When the respondent finishes, Growth Flow treats the record as a trigger: enrich the profile, segment it, and message them. Research Flow is a different loop: the AI drafts the study, recruits or uses the customer’s own audience, moderates many interviews in parallel, and returns summaries rather than a raw spreadsheet. Integrations are advertised as a stack connection, not as the product itself.

The page does not describe a human inbox over live submissions. “Real time” here means enrichment, notifications, and workflow triggers after the answer is in.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Typeform has already paid to teach the market that one-question forms finish more often, and it is now bolting automation and AI interviews onto that habit. Pulsarform should not compete on “prettier conversation.” The open lane is what happens to a single live submission as a thread a person or a machine can answer inside, rather than a flow that fires and leaves.

Use their numbers as the incumbent baseline (2x completion, 3.5x data, 48 million responses a month) and then move the story to the moment after submit. Research Flow shows buyers will pay for forms that talk back, but that talk is a moderated study, not an operational inbox.
