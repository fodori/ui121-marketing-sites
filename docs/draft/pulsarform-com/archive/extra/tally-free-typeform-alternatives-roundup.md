## Summary

Marie Martens, 5 August 2026, writes for people who liked Typeform’s feel and hit a cap, a paywall, or a bill. The evaluation criteria match her other roundups: real free limits, non-technical editing, free integrations, and brand control. The shortlist again puts Tally first for unlimited responses and a document editor, Jotform second for templates and integrations, and Google Forms third for the Google account you already have. The fetched text is the introduction and the top picks, not a full matrix. It overlaps her longer Typeform-alternatives guide and the best-free roundup. The new emphasis is emotional: Typeform made forms pleasant, and pleasant became expensive. She does not argue that the one-question format should die. She argues you can get interactive forms without that price.

## Key facts

- Updated 5 August 2026. Author: Marie Martens.
- Audience: Typeform users blocked by caps or price.
- Top picks named in the extract: Tally, Jotform, Google Forms.
- Same scoring axes as the other Tally roundups.
- Overlaps the longer alternatives article. Not an independent dataset.

## How it works

Switch builders, keep a conversational or branded form, collect without Typeform’s response meter. Post-submit behavior is out of scope in the portion fetched.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

The switching trigger in this category is a bill, not a missing inbox. Pulsarform’s landing page should not lead with “cheaper than Typeform.” It should assume people already know forms can feel like a conversation, then show the conversation continuing after submit, which none of these free alternatives advertise.
