## Summary

This URL is a thin feature card inside Jotform Workflows, not a full product story. The fetched page pairs two surfaces. Jotform Tables tracks every workflow run in a spreadsheet-style view: who acted, where the run stands, and follow-ups from one grid. Jotform Inbox is the threaded alternative: a real-time view of each workflow run where you see who did what, and when, and you take action from the inbox itself.

That is the closest public wording in this research set to a live machine inbox. It is also buried. The page has almost no mechanism, no screenshots described in text, no pricing, and no respondent-facing reply. The San Francisco office address is the only other durable fact on the extract. The inbox here is a workflow side-feature: it watches a run that started from a form, it does not redefine the form as the channel.

## Key facts

- Inbox: threaded, real-time view of each workflow run.
- Actions are taken from the inbox, not only observed.
- Tables is the sibling view: spreadsheet of who acted and current stage.
- Page depth is a feature blurb, not documentation.
- Positioned under Products → Workflows, not as the form builder’s home.

## How it works

A form submission starts a workflow. As people approve, assign, or follow up, Inbox appends those events into one thread per run. Tables shows the same runs as rows. The page does not say whether the original respondent sees any of this, or whether a reply goes back into the form.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Quote the “who did what, when” idea as market validation, then stress the placement: Jotform hid it under workflow features. Pulsarform can make that thread the front door. The form builder and the inbox are the same product, and the respondent can still be in the thread after submit.

Do not over-claim Jotform lacks an inbox. They shipped the metaphor. They did not lead with it.
