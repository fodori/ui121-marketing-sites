## Summary

This URL is an article collection, not a single post. It introduces Tally as the form builder with unlimited forms and unlimited submissions for free, then sends readers into detailed comparisons against Notion Forms, Typeform, Google Forms, Jotform, Paperform, Fillout, Microsoft Forms, and Formstack. Those child pages are saved separately under archive/extra/. The hub also links feature explainers (conditional logic, file uploads, payments, custom domains, customization) and roundups (best free builders, MCP builders, GDPR builders, Typeform alternatives, and a few integration landing pages). This note covers the hub itself. Child articles are not pasted here.

The hub’s own matrix, which should be treated as Tally’s marketing and checked before it is repeated as fact, contrasts four columns: Tally, Typeform, Jotform, and Google Forms. On that table, Tally’s free plan is unlimited responses, while Typeform’s free plan is 10 responses a month and Jotform’s is 100. File uploads are free on Tally at 10MB per file. Conditional logic is free on Tally and Typeform, and the table marks Jotform’s as paid (other Tally pages in this set disagree and mark Jotform logic as free, so do not treat this cell as settled). A formula editor is free on Tally, paid on Typeform, widget-only on Jotform, absent on Google Forms. Payments are free on Tally via its Stripe path, paid on Typeform and Jotform, and third-party only on Google. Custom domains are $29 a month on Tally versus much higher tiers elsewhere. Remove branding is $29 on Tally, about $79 on Typeform, about $34 on Jotform. Submission retention control is $89 on Tally and marked unavailable on the other three. MCP is free on Tally, Typeform, and Jotform, and absent on Google Forms. AI form building is free on Tally and Jotform, paid on Typeform, and tied to a paid Google tier. User ratings on the table: Tally 4.8/5, Typeform 4.5, Jotform 4.4, Google Forms 4.6. A later child page cites Tally at 4.9 on G2, so the hub’s 4.8 and the child pages’ 4.9 should not be merged into one number.

FAQ positions: Google Forms and Tally are the truly unlimited free options, with Tally claiming more advanced features. Typeform is still the one-question format people recognize. Jotform is the enterprise and template choice, not the design-flexible one. Tally Pro at $29 is presented as cheaper than Typeform at $39 for 100 responses, Jotform at $34 for 1,000, or Paperform at $20 with limits. Import is one click. Security claims: EU hosting, GDPR, password protection and 2FA on the free plan. Accessibility: semantic HTML, keyboard and screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA) on every plan.

No pagination controls appeared on the fetched page. Extra notes cover the comparison articles and a capped set of linked guides.

## Key facts

- Hub, not a single article. Eight named comparison children.
- Free-plan response caps as Tally states them here: Tally unlimited, Typeform 10/month, Jotform 100/month, Google Forms unlimited.
- Tally Pro cited at $29/month for branding, domains, and similar.
- Retention control cited at $89/month, claimed unique in this four-way table.
- MCP marked free on Tally, Typeform, and Jotform.
- Ratings on this table: 4.8 / 4.5 / 4.4 / 4.6.
- Accessibility claim: keyboard and screen readers on the free plan.
- Internal contradiction to watch: conditional logic paywall for Jotform differs across Tally pages.

## How it works

The page is a directory. A reader picks a competitor, opens a feature table and a pricing story, and is pushed toward creating a Tally form or importing an old one. The mechanism of Tally itself is not re-explained beyond “unlimited and free, professional features included.” Distribution of submissions is out of scope here.

## Implications for pulsarform.com / ui121

Use this hub as the category map, not as a spec. The axes competitors fight on are price caps, one-question versus document editing, templates, payments, and GDPR. None of the eight comparison titles is “who answers the submission live.” That absence is the positioning gap. When Pulsarform publishes its own comparisons, lead with the post-submit thread, and only then show builder table stakes so the page does not look naive.
