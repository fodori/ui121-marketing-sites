- **Source:** https://rentahuman.ai/

- **Status:** ok

## Summary

RentAHuman presents itself as the physical-world workforce for AI. The pitch is that software cannot act in the real world, so an agent or a person rents a human to do the thing and bring back evidence. The homepage hero counted 826,211 “rentable humans.” A FAQ on the same page said the marketplace is live with 787,000+ registered humans in 100+ countries and that tasks complete every day. Those two headcount figures do not match; use both only as “high hundreds of thousands,” and do not treat either as an audited census.

The offer is meatspace work, not expert Q&A. Featured jobs are street marketing, sign holding, flyer drops, pop-ups, and recurring human user tests that return evidence. Sample tasks on the page are priced as fixed fees: a cross-town birthday gift at $45, a 60-second product video at $150, coffee-shop reviews at $200, a market booth at $300, a week of dog walking at $175. Locations on the samples include Los Angeles, San Francisco, New York, and Chicago, plus remote video work. One sample is attributed to an agent-style poster (“MarketBot”), which is the product story in miniature: software posts the job.

The buyer loop is browse, request or message, human does the work with live updates, then pay only when satisfied. Trust modules are escrow-style hold, verified status, recent activity, and reviews. A business lane invites local companies to plug in so AI traffic can reach them. An agent lane points to MCP and a REST API.

> “Your AI can't touch grass.” — RentAHuman homepage

## Key facts

- Hero count on this fetch: 826,211 rentable humans. FAQ count: 787,000+ across 100+ countries.
- Sample fixed prices: $45, $150, $175, $200, $300.
- Use cases shown: in-person marketing, scheduled human product testing, errands, video, pet care.
- Agent path advertised: MCP server or REST API, with a link to /for-agents.
- Payments described as funds held until the buyer is satisfied.
- This fetch did not show a dollar-paid total or press logos; do not cite those from this page.

## How it works

1. Browse humans by skill, location, and rate with no account.
2. Either message someone or post a task with a budget so humans apply.
3. The human follows instructions and sends updates while the work happens off-screen.
4. Funds release when the buyer accepts the result.

Agents are told they can search, post, and manage hiring without a person driving the UI. The page does not describe a question-and-answer object, a per-minute expert clock, or a structured answer card.

## Implications for helpexpert.me / newhuman.store

This is the live incumbent for “AI hires a human.” It owns the physical and proof-of-completion story. A deferred knowledge product should not copy “touch grass” or street-team examples. The opening is what the homepage never sells: a skilled person answering a question in minutes, with a structured result an agent can consume. Borrow the loop (match, evidence, held payment) and change the unit of work from an errand to a judgment. The conflicting headcount is a reminder to quote a range, not a precise vanity number, if we ever compare scale.
