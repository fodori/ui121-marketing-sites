## Summary

Typeform’s own guide, last published 13 December 2024 in the byline (schema modified the same day), argues that conversations produce better customer insight than a broadcast, and that most brands still monologue. The business stats they stack: 86% of buyers will pay more for a great experience, 70% will buy only from brands that understand them, and 66% expect brands to know their preferences. More than two-thirds of consumers in a Salesforce survey they cite feel treated as a number, and over 60% feel companies show no empathy.

The strategic move is zero-party data: what people volunteer. They call it more accurate, more surprising, and more transparent than cookies. Dialogue builds profiles cookies cannot. Two stories carry the middle. Calm replaced a pushy homepage with a conversational poll about the visitor’s own needs, and uses in-app polls so research checks whether the product still matches real problems. They quote Chase Clark, then Senior UX Researcher at Calm, on keeping a pulse, and note Calm’s public scale as they state it: top sleep app, over 100 million downloads, 1.5 million-plus five-star reviews. Agexa built a poll for Credit Stacking that asked for a credit score so leads over 650 could be routed differently. Sam Queen at Agexa told Typeform the close rate moved from 10% to 18% “pretty much overnight.”

A branding stat: 96% of companies that use conversational forms say the forms strengthen the brand (Typeform’s research, as claimed here). Scale is the problem. You cannot interview every lead, so the form has to mimic a conversation using three features. Logic and branching send people down different paths (their magazine example: The New Yorker versus Time). Recall pulls an earlier answer into a later question so nobody repeats themselves. Name personalization uses that recall so the form says the person’s name.

The page’s related tips in September 2026 (forced-choice questions, suggestion boxes, quota sampling) show the blog is still a research-methods hub. The article itself stops at designing the form. It does not describe a human picking up the thread after submit.

## How it works

Write a short conversational form. Branch on answers so each person sees a relevant path. Recall earlier answers, including the name. Use the path to score and route the lead into a different follow-up. The “two-way street” they invoke is still a scripted form, not an open reply. Revenue comes from better zero-party profiles and tighter qualification, then ordinary marketing automation.
