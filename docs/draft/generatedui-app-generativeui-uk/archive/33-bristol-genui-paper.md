## Summary

The Bristol research-information page is a publication record, not the paper. It lists a conference paper by Rachel Eardley and Emma L. Tonkin, from Engineering Mathematics and Technology and Computer Science. Status: published 15 April 2026. Event: the workshop “What does Generative UI mean for HCI Practice?”, colocated with ACM CHI 2026 in Barcelona, 15 April 2026. Links from the record: a Microsoft Research publication page for the workshop, and https://genuimeetshci.github.io/chi26-workshop/.

The abstract is short. The authors, writing as a UX designer and a governance researcher, observed a design process. They describe generative AI and generative UI as tools that can support practice and also as “quixotic polishers of incomplete concepts.” Their conclusion: the approach is powerful, and designers still have to keep accountability and take on the duties the method creates, including requirements that get skipped — coherent information architecture, and consistent attention to regulatory requirements.

The full paper is not on this page. No method section, quotes from sessions, or figures were available. This note stops at the abstract and the bibliographic facts.

## How it works

No system is specified. The mechanism in the abstract is social: generative tools sit inside a design process, speed or polish the output, and can hide gaps in structure and compliance unless a designer remains responsible for those gaps.

## Implications for generatedui / ui121

The title is the message for a UK audience that fears designers are obsolete. generatedui should say the catalog and the rules are design work: information architecture becomes the block model and the data bindings; regulation becomes what a block is allowed to collect and submit. The agent arranges; it does not own accountability. That matches human-in-the-loop approvals and a pre-built catalog better than free-form page generation, which is exactly where incomplete concepts get a polished surface.
